## Findings, verdict and recommendation

Hand-written after reviewing the tables above and a few one-off checks, which are noted inline.

### Summary

- **FC 25, FC 26 and FC 27 can be added.** All three have every top-5 club with a full squad. Every one of the
  96 clubs per version passes `best_xi.py`, with at most one `adjacent` fit in total. All three have full position
  lists, nationality, all six face stats and GK stats, and **EA/SoFIFA player ids, which are the same ids we use
  today**: 99.8–100% of the ids that overlap with our data belong to the same person.
- **FIFA 10–14 are not feasible from the Futhead scrape alone.** It has **no goalkeepers at all**, its **six stats
  are scrambled** by a scraper bug, it has one position per card, and it has no nationality and no ids. Its ratings
  are usable, but extracting base cards gets the exact rating right for about 90–95% of players, and some star
  players come out wrong.
- **FIFA 09:** no data in any source.

### A. FC 25, FC 26, FC 27

1. **Snapshot = launch.**
   - **FC 25:** EA ratings site, packaged on 2024-09-26, the day before release. 10/10 spot checks match (7 that I'm
     confident of, plus Salah 89, van Dijk 89 and Yamal 81).
   - **FC 26:** SoFIFA, `fifa_update` 4 dated 2025-09-19, the first day of early access. 10/10 values match what I
     recall, but I'm not certain of those recollections, so they're marked uncertain.
   - **FC 27:** EA ratings API snapshot from **2026-09-12**. That is in the pre-release reveal window, and it is
     **after my knowledge cutoff, so I cannot verify any FC 27 rating or transfer**. Two values look surprising
     but may well be correct: Salah at Trabzonspor (87) and Rodri at FC Barcelona. The dataset's README says new
     snapshots follow "through the reveal window, launch day, and later title updates".
2. **Top 5 and squads.** Every version has 20/20/20/18/18 clubs (EPL/LaLiga/Serie A/Bundesliga/Ligue 1). Squads
   have 20–38 players, every club has a GK, and `best_xi.py` passes for 96/96 clubs everywhere.
3. **Ids.**
   - **Players:** 94.6% (FC 25), 82.8% (FC 26) and 73.9% (FC 27) of top-5 players already exist in our FIFA 15–FC 24
     data under the same id, and the names agree for 99.8–100%. The rest are mostly new young players. Players
     keep their identity across versions, which matters for later ideas such as B24 All-time.
   - **Clubs, FC 26:** `club_team_id` is our `team_id` (96/96 exist).
   - **Clubs, FC 25 and FC 27:** there is no club id, and EA uses its own names. A majority vote of the
     players' FC 24 clubs maps all 96 FC 25 clubs. FC 27 reuses FC 25's EA names for 75 clubs, and the other 21
     (promoted since) map through FC 26. The pipeline would keep a small alias table (EA name → `team_id`).
   - **EA's unlicensed names:** these sources use EA's in-game names: "Lombardia FC" (Inter), "Milano FC" (Milan),
     "Bergamo Calcio" (Atalanta), "Latium" (Lazio). With the alias table we can show the real names, as today
     (D26).
4. **Gaps against our JSON format** (each is small):

| field | FC 25 (EA site) | FC 26 (SoFIFA) | FC 27 (EA API) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | from the URL | `player_id` | `player_id` |
| `name` / `fullName` | one display name ("Kylian Mbappé"); derive a short form or use it for both | `short_name` / `long_name` as today | `common_name`, or first + last name |
| `nation` / `nationCode` | 97.7% in `nations.py`; add Holland, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Guinea-Bissau | 98.4%; add Türkiye, Czechia, Cabo Verde, Guinea-Bissau, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia | 97.0%; same additions as FC 25 |
| `age` | `Age` | `age` | from `birthdate` and the release date |
| `positions` | `Position` + `Alternative positions` | `player_positions` | `position` + `alternate_positions` |
| `stats` / `gk` | all present; GK speed = the GK's `PAC` (assumed, not verified) | all present, incl. `goalkeeping_speed` | all present; GK speed = the GK's `pace` (assumed) |
| `eaPos`, `eaFormation`, EA-XI tie-break | **missing** (no EA team sheet) | present (`club_position`) | **missing** |
| team `ovr` (sorting) | missing; use the XI average | `male_teams` not in the download; use the XI average | missing; use the XI average |

   `eaPos` only feeds the "was in EA's XI" tie-break and `eaFormation`, which nothing displays yet. Without it,
   the tie-break simply moves on to the lower `player_id`.

### B. FIFA 10–14 (Futhead scrape, kafagy/fifa-FUT-Data)

1. **Two hard blockers:**
   - **No goalkeepers.** There are zero GK cards in all eleven files, FIFA 10 to FIFA 20. The GK slot can't be
     filled from this source.
   - **The stats are unusable.** `futhead.py` builds the stat groups with `Stats.index(stat)`. BeautifulSoup tags
     compare equal by content, so `index()` returns the first equal-looking stat on the page and the groups slip.
     The zip of cards and stats then pairs cards with another card's stats. Against our SoFIFA launch data for the
     same FIFA 15–19 players, the six stats are within 6 points in total for only 0.1% of base cards, and more than
     20 points off for about 97%. That holds even for FIFA 19, where ratings match 99.7%. Example: Hazard's
     FIFA 15 base card carries DEF 95.
2. **Ratings are usable, with errors.**
   - **Ratings and stats:** player names and ratings come from separate lists that line up, so ratings are reliable
     even where stats are not.
   - **Proposed base-card rule:**
     - drop exact duplicates;
     - keep the lowest-rated card per (name, club);
     - drop a club where that name has only higher-rated cards, which is a special card issued after a transfer.
   - **On FutBin FIFA 19** (which has a card-type column), this picks 2,847 cards with 99.7% precision and 98.6%
     recall.
   - **On the Futhead files, which have no card type,** the exact rating matches SoFIFA launch for 89.8–94.9% of
     FIFA 15–18 players (95.5–98.3% within ±1). FIFA 19 scores 99.7%, but it was scraped before launch, when no
     special cards existed.
   - **Where the rule fails:** the misses go both ways (about 4–7% lower, 1–5% higher). There are lower-rated cards
     that are not the base card: FIFA 13 Messi has a 91 card below his 94 base, and FIFA 14 Ibrahimović an 89
     below a 90. Some base cards are also missing, so a special card is picked instead. FIFA 11–14 were scraped
     at the same time as FIFA 15–18 (2018-09-19, long after each season ended), so expect the same 90–95%.
3. **Detecting special cards:** every card above the lowest one at a club is treated as special. Note that the
   files also contain whole non-club leagues, such as FIFA 14's 969 `World Cup` cards; the top-5 league filter
   removes those.
4. **Winter-transfer cards:** a player who moved mid-season has a base card with the same rating at both clubs.
   This affects 0, 1, 175, 249 and 335 names in FIFA 10–14, mostly in FIFA 12–14. The launch club can't be read
   from the data. One heuristic: if the next version's club is one of the two, the launch club is probably the
   other. That isn't reliable, because players also move in the summer. Unresolved, these players would appear
   in two teams of the same version, which `validate.py` rejects.
5. **Nationality:** there is no column. Matching names to our FIFA 15–FC 24 players fills 61% (FIFA 10), 64%
   (11), 71% (12), 77% (13) and 82% (14) of base cards. The rest are players who retired before 2014; they would
   have no flag.
6. **Ids:** name matches to FIFA 15 cover 58–81% of base cards, and only unique name matches count. There are no
   club ids, so mapping would be by club name; Futhead uses yet another naming scheme, e.g. "F.C. Barcelona" and
   "Real Madrid C.F." in FIFA 10.
7. **Single position:** one position per card. With a placeholder GK, the outfield part passes `best_xi.py` for
   98/98 clubs in FIFA 11–14, with 11–39 `adjacent` fits per version. That is more than today, because nobody has
   a second position.
8. **FIFA 10:**
   - **Incomplete.** It has gold cards only (rated 75 or more, 1,340 cards in total), and squads of 1–22 players.
     Even with a placeholder GK, only 38 of 96 clubs pass `best_xi.py`.
   - **Uncertain ratings.** FIFA 10's Ultimate Team was a paid add-on released in spring 2010, so its "launch"
     ratings are not the game's launch ratings. 0 of the 5 spot checks match my recollection (Ronaldo 93,
     Gerrard 90), but my FIFA 10 memory is not reliable.

**Verdict on source B:** FIFA 11–14 could only be included as a reduced game: no GK round, no stats on the cards,
about a third of players without a flag, ratings correct for about 9 in 10 players, and the transfer duplicates
resolved by hand. I don't recommend that. FIFA 10 is not feasible from it. Source B is only worth reconsidering
alongside another source for FIFA 10–14 that provides GKs and stats. A SoFIFA-format export for those years would
be ideal (see the notes below).

the Kaggle sets cover FC 25–27 only. That matches the expectation in B1.

### E. Recommended order

1. **FC 26.** Same SoFIFA schema and ids as today, so it's the quickest win: mostly a loader switch
   (`fifa_update_date`, a single file) plus deriving team `ovr`.
2. **FC 25.** One new loader for EA-format data, plus the club alias table and nation aliases. Both are reused
   by FC 27.
3. **FC 27.** The same loader with different column names. Prefer a launch-day snapshot if one is published.
   Otherwise the 2026-09-12 data is fine for the MVP; reveal ratings rarely change before launch.
4. **FIFA 10–14:** park until a source with GKs and correct stats is found. Planning would have to decide whether a
   reduced mode is worth it.

Pipeline changes needed for 1–3 (for the integration stages, not done here):
- **Per-source loaders** that produce today's player frame.
- **Nation aliases or new codes:** Holland → `nl`, Türkiye → `tr`, Czechia → `cz`, Cabo Verde → `cv`,
  Guinea-Bissau → `gw`, plus Saudi Arabia `sa`, Indonesia `id` and Malaysia `my`.
- **Club handling:** an EA club alias table, and team `ovr` derived from the XI average.
- **Version labels:** "EA SPORTS FC 25/26/27".

## Assumptions not in the prompt

- I evaluated the EA-format sources at the level of top-5 men's clubs only, and used the men's rows where a file
  mixes men and women.
- **EA league names** identify the top 5 in FC 25 and FC 27. "Bundesliga" is unique there; Austria is
  "Ö. Bundesliga".
- **Name matching** (Futhead → SoFIFA) uses normalised full, short and first-plus-last names. Only unique matches
  are counted, so the match rates are conservative.
- **The 40% majority threshold** for mapping clubs by their players is my choice.
- **The FutBin FIFA 19 file** from the same repository was used only to measure the base-card rule, never as data
  for the game.
